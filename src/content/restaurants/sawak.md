Sawak Cafe is one of those places Hobart should be proud of. Tucked on Collins Street, it's been quietly serving some of the most authentic Malaysian street food in Tasmania since 2010 — not chasing trends, not dressing things up, just cooking the food that owner and chef Zye Chong grew up eating in Sarawak.

The room is simple and unpretentious. Sarawak wall hangings, open kitchen, flaming woks — you can watch the cook happening in real time. Help yourself to napkins, utensils and bottled water; the chilli sauces on the table do the rest. It has the feel of a place that earns its reputation through food alone, which it does.

The **laksa** is the headline act — and deservedly so. It won Tassie's laksa category in the *Delicious* 100 for 2023, and it's not hard to see why. A deep coconut milk broth, chilli oil floating on top, vermicelli, tofu puffs, a hard-boiled egg, beansprouts and coriander, with your choice of protein. It's the kind of bowl that makes a cold Hobart day bearable.

The **har mee prawn soup** is equally serious — rich, sweet prawn broth with real depth, the sort you don't find outside of a Malaysian household or a place like this. The **wonton soup** is comforting and clean. Portions are generous and prices are genuinely reasonable for the quality.

Worth noting: Sawak has been featured in Gourmet Traveller, The Mercury, and listed among Hobart's best cheap eats by Tourism Australia. The kind of accolades that actually mean something when the place has been around for over a decade.

**Verdict:** If you're ever in Hobart and want the real thing — not a watered-down approximation of Malaysian food — this is it.
